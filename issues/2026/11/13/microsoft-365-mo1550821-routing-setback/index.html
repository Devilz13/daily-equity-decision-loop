Issue 06013 November 2026

Microsoft 365 outage continues after recovery changes fall short

Evidence reviewed at 12:00 SGT on 13 November 2026. Service degradation continues.

What changed

Earlier restarts showed promise. In the next update, timestamped 10:58 SGT (02:58 UTC) in a public service-health mirror, Microsoft said the changes did not take effect as expected and it was looking at other ways to add capacity. At 11:36 SGT (03:36 UTC) in the same mirror, it reported that persistent traffic imbalances were under investigation and it was reviewing traffic flows and infrastructure to identify better routes. The Exchange Online notice mirrored by Penn also reports the setback and routing work. Microsoft 365 notice as mirrored by 365Fanatic · University of Pennsylvania mirror of Exchange Online service health.

What is still affected

Both the broader Microsoft 365 incident MO1550821 and related Exchange Online incident EX1550543 are listed as degraded in the available incident records. Cisco ThousandEyes independently observed widespread Outlook connection timeouts and continuing packet loss in Microsoft’s network at 11:00 SGT (03:00 UTC). Outlook mailbox access and mail delivery can still be affected; certain Teams calendar and scheduling features depend on Exchange Online. These observations do not establish a new Singapore-specific impact or a platform-wide Azure outage. Independent network measurements · Microsoft 365 service scope.

What remains unknown

The earlier notices identified an overloaded server process handling secure connections. The new updates describe a recovery setback and routing work; they do not establish the underlying trigger. Microsoft said in the mirrored Exchange notice that it had no indication of malicious activity. No full-restoration time was confirmed. A further Exchange status update was listed by 12:30 SGT (04:30 UTC); this was not a restoration estimate. Exchange Online incident chronology.

What to do now

If email or meeting scheduling fails and the matter is urgent, use an approved alternate channel and confirm receipt. Check Microsoft’s public service health page or your IT team for current status. Read the earlier recovery update.
