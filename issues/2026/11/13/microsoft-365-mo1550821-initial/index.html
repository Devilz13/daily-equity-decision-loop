Issue 05813 November 2026

Microsoft 365 outage: recovery underway, full restoration unconfirmed

Evidence checked 10:02 SGT on 13 November 2026. Partial recovery; full restoration and root cause unconfirmed.

Who is affected

Microsoft 365 customers may see Outlook or Exchange mailbox access failures, Teams problems, and degraded Microsoft 365 Copilot and some SharePoint functionality. A public university bulletin also lists Defender XDR; the exact symptom varies by service. Some functions can remain available, such as files stored directly in SharePoint Online according to a mirrored vendor update. Global reach was independently observed, but this does not show that all users, all Azure services, or Singapore users specifically were affected. THERS university consortium: Microsoft incident MO1550821 · Cisco ThousandEyes: independent availability observations · Public status mirror: mitigation and service scope.

When it started and what changed

The broader Microsoft 365 incident MO1550821 began at 13:26 EST on 12 November (18:26 UTC; 02:26 SGT on 13 November), according to contemporaneous reporting citing Microsoft. Exchange Online mailbox problems were reported earlier under EX1550543. Cisco ThousandEyes independently observed Microsoft 365 availability issues from around 17:30 UTC (01:30 SGT); that observation is not the vendor’s formal broader-incident start. BleepingComputer: Microsoft statements and incident start · Earlham College: Exchange Online and Microsoft 365 incident IDs · Cisco ThousandEyes: independent availability observations.

Some restarted servers were stable during limited recovery, but others stopped responding when traffic returned. Microsoft has shifted traffic, restarted infrastructure, restricted some traffic temporarily and tested rolling back a recent network configuration change. These are mitigations under evaluation. Full restoration and a dependable ETA were not verified at the stated check. The Business Times, updated 08:27 SGT · THERS university consortium: Microsoft incident MO1550821 · Public status mirror: mitigation and service scope.

What we know about the cause

Microsoft described an overwhelmed server process and loss of capacity after servers became unavailable. A recent network configuration change is being examined. A correlation, or improvement after rollback, is not by itself a confirmed root cause. There is no basis in the cited updates to call this a cyberattack. BleepingComputer: Microsoft statements and incident start · THERS university consortium: Microsoft incident MO1550821 · Public status mirror: mitigation and service scope.

What to do now

For urgent messages, use an alternate channel approved by your employer and confirm receipt. Check your IT team or Microsoft public service health; an administrator can look up the two incident IDs in the Microsoft 365 admin center. We’ll distinguish partial recovery from a confirmed all-clear.
