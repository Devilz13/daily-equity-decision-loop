Issue 06214 November 2026

Microsoft identifies a network upgrade issue and reports broad recovery

Evidence checked at 03:06 SGT on 14 November 2026. Broad recovery; no all-clear.

What went wrong

In notices posted at about 01:57 SGT on 14 November (17:57 UTC on 13 November), Microsoft attributed the disruption to a problem introduced during an earlier network capacity upgrade. A more recent network change exposed that problem. Earlier notices described overloaded server processes and traffic imbalances; Microsoft’s latest explanation identifies where the problem began. Microsoft said it was a technical issue unrelated to a cyberattack or AI agents. The detailed post-incident report has not been published in the sources reviewed. Microsoft service-health notices mirrored by PennO365.

What Microsoft has fixed

Microsoft said it addressed the underlying issue and restored service gradually, checking stability at each stage. It reported broad recovery while work continued for remaining customers and regions. Associated Press reported improving capacity and mail queues approaching normal levels, with some intermittent access or delayed messages. Cisco ThousandEyes independently measured widespread Outlook timeouts at 11:00 SGT on 13 November; that earlier measurement does not establish overnight conditions. Mirrored service notices · Associated Press coverage · Earlier independent network measurements.

What is still open

The available notices still listed both the broader Microsoft 365 incident MO1550821 and the Exchange Online incident EX1550543 as degraded. Outlook and Exchange access and delivery, and Exchange-dependent functions in other Microsoft 365 services, may still be affected. The notices listed a further update at 04:00 SGT on 14 November (20:00 UTC on 13 November), not a promised recovery time. For urgent work, use an approved alternate channel and confirm receipt.
