Issue 06314 November 2026

Microsoft marks the Microsoft 365 and Exchange Online disruption resolved

Evidence checked at 13:05 SGT on 14 November 2026. Both incident IDs now show restored.

What Microsoft confirmed

Microsoft set Exchange Online incident EX1550543 to restored at 12:15:46 SGT and the broader Microsoft 365 incident MO1550821 to restored at 12:17:52 SGT on 14 November (04:15:46 and 04:17:52 UTC). These are the closure notice times. The notices still mention work for remaining customers and regions; they do not establish when the last affected customer recovered or when every queued email was delivered. Microsoft service-health notices mirrored by PennO365 · Microsoft public service health.

When it happened

Both closing notices retrospectively list an impact start of 18:05 SGT on 12 November (10:05 UTC), earlier than the start fields in the initial advisories. Exchange Online lists an impact end of 00:10 SGT on 14 November (16:10 UTC on 13 November). The Microsoft 365 notice body gives that same end time, but its structured field in the PennO365 mirror says 12:10 SGT on 13 November (04:10 UTC). That discrepancy remains unresolved; there is no independently verified single end time for the wider incident.

During the disruption, Microsoft redirected traffic, restarted affected infrastructure and restored capacity in stages, checking stability as it went. At 22:00 SGT on 13 November (14:00 UTC), ThousandEyes observed that Outlook was largely reachable again, with packet loss or timeouts still present in some locations. At about 10:13–10:17 SGT on 14 November (02:13–02:17 UTC), Microsoft was still monitoring queued mail and remaining service issues. The restoration notices followed at 12:15–12:17 SGT. ThousandEyes network measurements · Mirrored service notices.

What was affected

The disruption affected users across multiple regions, including the Americas, Europe and Asia-Pacific, with impact varying by customer and function. Exchange Online users could have trouble opening Outlook mailboxes or experience delayed email; hybrid mail flow could also be affected. The broader incident included Exchange-dependent Teams calendar and meeting functions and some SharePoint, Copilot and Defender features. This does not establish a worldwide Azure outage. In Singapore, GovTech had reported disruption to some government email services while Singpass and emergency services remained operational. The Microsoft closing notices do not give a separate Singapore restoration time. Associated Press coverage · Earlier Singapore bulletin.

Why it happened

Microsoft’s preliminary explanation is that an earlier network capacity upgrade introduced a problem that surfaced after a later network change. Microsoft says it fixed the underlying technical problem and that the incident was unrelated to cyber activity or AI agents. It plans a preliminary post-incident report within two business days and a final report within five; neither is part of the evidence for this bulletin.
