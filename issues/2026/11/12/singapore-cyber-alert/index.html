Singapore data breach alert: what to do if someone calls or messages you

Special public safety bulletin · 12 November 2026 at 14:02 SGT. Separate from the daily market edition.

What to do firstWhich organisationsProtect your accountsSources

What to do first

  1. Hang up. A caller who knows your name, NRIC or booking details may still be a scammer. Open the app you already use, or call the number on your bank card, bill or official website. Do not call a number supplied by the caller or message.
  2. Keep codes private. Never tell anyone your one-time password (OTP), PIN, password or Singpass code. Never approve a login or payment you did not start. Do not scan a QR code, install an app or share your phone screen because a caller tells you to.
  3. If you paid, shared bank details or approved a login: call your bank immediately on the number on your card or in its official app. Ask it to block suspicious transactions and use the bank’s emergency lock if available. Save the messages and receipts and file a police report. Do not wait for a company to reply.
  4. Unsure? Call ScamShield at 1799. It is available 24 hours. You can ask a trusted family member to sit with you while you check. ScamShield: Call 1799 if unsure · ScamShield: If you have been scammed.

What was reported, and what to watch for

Dates distinguish discovery from disclosure where known. These are separate incidents unless a source identifies a supplier relationship. A notification does not mean money was stolen; stolen details can make later scams more believable. No evidence reviewed establishes one attacker across all the organisations.

Insurance · Alliance Medinet

Disclosed 24 Oct; company update 26 Oct. About 110,000 members were reported affected at the healthcare benefits administrator. Potential exposure included names, ID numbers, dates of birth, contact and policy details. Some employer-linked plans were involved, including policies from Singlife, Income, HSBC Life, Great Eastern, Cigna and Tokio Marine as reported. This does not mean every customer of those insurers was affected. The company said fixes to the access route were independently validated and its other systems were unaffected.

Watch for: Fake claim payouts, policy renewals or requests to update bank details. Check with your insurer using your policy document or official app.

Alliance Healthcare Group’s follow-up filing · Reported Alliance Medinet scope and affected insurers · Singlife notice on Alliance Medinet

Singapore Airlines · Amadeus

SIA disclosed the impact on 3 Nov. About 46,000 booking records included names, booking references and travel details. About 620 records may also have included contact, travel document or KrisFlyer details. SIA said the incident involved its booking vendor Amadeus and its own IT systems were not breached. Amadeus said no bank or card data was exposed and no bookings were changed as a result.

Watch for: Fake flight changes, refunds or mileage notices. Open the airline app or type its website to check Manage Booking. Do not share booking references or travel documents in unsolicited chats.

Singapore Airlines statement reported by CNA · Amadeus incident statement

M1

Detected 30 Oct, disclosed 6 Nov. Names, NRIC/FIN, birthdays, phone numbers, emails and addresses were affected. M1 said it contained the breach and that customer account access, payment card and banking information were not compromised.

Watch for: Fake bill, refund or SIM replacement notices. Use the M1 app or the number on your bill. If your phone suddenly loses service and you suspect a SIM change, contact M1 from another phone.

M1 data breach disclosure

Doctor Anywhere · Adept Health / Income

Disclosed 6 Nov. Doctor Anywhere said personal information was exposed and clinical records were unaffected on the evidence then available; it did not publish a full field list. Separately, Income said it was notified on 2 Nov by its administrator Adept Health, and that the affected Income group may include identification, policy, contact and bank account details. Income’s notice is not a field list for every Doctor Anywhere user.

Watch for: Fake clinic invoices, insurer reimbursements or bank detail change requests. Check the provider or insurer directly; monitor your bank account if your notice mentions bank details.

Doctor Anywhere statement reported by CNA · Income Insurance notice on Adept Health

Fullerton Health

Identified 31 Oct, disclosed 9 Nov. Fullerton says a server for its Migrant Worker Primary Care Plan was affected. Possible data included names, FINs, birth dates and contact details. Its preliminary notice excludes medical and financial information, says care continued, and reports no evidence of impact to other programmes.

Watch for: Fake employer or clinic calls asking for identity details or a payment. Verify through the clinic or known employer contact.

Fullerton Health notice

SPH Media

Detected 4–8 Nov; disclosed 10 Nov. Five systems were attacked. About 9% of the database was affected, mainly email addresses plus some names, mailing addresses and phone numbers. SPH Media says no payment card, banking or customer account access was compromised.

Watch for: Fake newspaper subscription renewals, refunds or payment updates. Use the subscription account you already know.

SPH Media’s official statement · SPH Media’s detection dates

SATS · Tappee school meals

Disclosed 10 Nov. The app used by four schools was affected. Data included student names and emails, concession card numbers, financial assistance status and some parent/guardian emails. A small number of records included dietary, allergy or body measurements. SATS said banking, card and student identity document details were not collected by the app; its own IT infrastructure was not compromised. The app was suspended at disclosure; meals continued.

Watch for: Fake top-ups or requests for school or child details. Check with the school office via its usual number or parent channel. Do not circulate children’s details.

SATS statement reported by CNA

Minmed

Disclosed 11 Nov. A standalone system supporting teleconsultation and screening was affected. Minmed said clinical records were unaffected and that its investigator validated fixes as of 5 Nov. Exact exposed fields and numbers were not established in the public statement reviewed.

Watch for: Fake appointment reminders, medical bills or screening refunds. Call the clinic through the number you already use.

Minmed statement reported by CNA

Dementia Singapore · CARA

Incident reported to have occurred 1 Nov, publicly reported 11 Nov. Some members received unauthorised notifications. Names, email addresses, phone numbers and membership IDs may have been affected; Dementia Singapore said NRICs were not affected.

Watch for: Ignore unexpected CARA messages that ask for details or money. With a trusted caregiver, update the CARA app through the official Apple App Store or Google Play Store, as the agency advised.

Dementia Singapore notice reported by CNA

MoneySmart · related notice

Discovered 29 Oct; publicly reported 3 Nov. The company confirmed some Singapore and Hong Kong customer data was copied; the reports reviewed do not establish every affected field or customer.

Watch for: Be alert to fake loan, cashback or credit card approval offers that ask for an advance payment. Verify through the official provider.

MoneySmart statement reported by CNA

Protect your accounts and help a family member

If you only received a notice and see no suspicious activity, monitor statements and use bank transaction alerts. If you gave out a password, change it through the official site from a trusted device and change it wherever you reused it. Start with email, then affected accounts. Turn on two-step sign-in. If you installed an app or allowed screen sharing at a caller’s request, disconnect that device from the internet and call your bank from another phone. ScamShield: Phishing scams · ScamShield: If you have been scammed.

For caregivers: save trusted numbers on the person’s phone, agree that unexpected payment requests get a second check, and never ask them to disclose a code to you. Share this guide, not breach letters, booking references or identity documents.

For organisations: isolate affected systems, preserve evidence, verify fixes with independent specialists, notify affected people clearly through known channels, and limit the data held by vendors. Keep discovery, data exposure and service restoration updates separate.
