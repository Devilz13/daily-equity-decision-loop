Cybersecurity · 2–7 November and the October IDC Frontier case

IDC Frontier, Japan: Disruption began around 03:40 JST on 7 October; the company disclosed unauthorised access that day, resumed its backup service on 18 October, and published investigation results on 27 October. The company identified 495 business and government customers in affected zones of East Japan Region 1; some virtual servers were stopped and encrypted. It found no evidence that data stored inside customer VMs was transferred out. Separately, it confirmed unauthorised portal queries affecting up to 71 organisations and up to four unauthorised password changes. Those findings concern different systems and do not contradict each other. Initial notice · Backup restart · Investigation. No direct Singapore impact or state attribution was verified.

Cold-chain cyberattacks, Europe · October–November; reported by SingCERT 6 NovemberSingCERT describes reported supermarket refrigeration disruptions and a separate reported French blood-service case linked to a supplier. Exact victim dates, total losses and attribution were not independently established. No confirmed direct Singapore impact. Check operator notices, abnormal temperatures and restoration; equipment failure is an alternative for an isolated alarm until logs establish a cyber cause.
4 November · Cisco ASA/FTD and MetabaseSingCERT alerts warn of active exploitation, with service denial and unauthorised administrator access as distinct risks. The alerts do not establish Singapore victims. Match versions against vendor notices, restrict exposure, patch and investigate abnormal logs.
2–3 November · Oracle and Palo AltoSingCERT alerts describe targeting of Oracle Application Object Library and reported exploitation affecting PAN-OS. Check whether deployed versions are affected; do not count alerts as separate local breaches.
6 November · personal AI agentsCSA, GovTech and IMDA advisory; safety guidance, not an attack. Limit permissions and require human approval for payments, external messages and sensitive files.

Continuity test: keep an independent offline or immutable backup under separate admin credentials and restore a representative service. Map common identity, backups and support portals before assuming two regions are independent. For cold chains, use independent alarms and safe local control where supported. By 9 November, look for scoped restoration or customer notices; a tested restore narrows availability risk, while new disclosure would widen the exposure assessment.

Singapore Ready Bag · cash, documents and a 30-minute build

SCDF lists cash as optional and does not prescribe an amount. De Nederlandsche Bank suggests €70 per adult and €30 per child for 72 hours; Sweden’s Riksbank suggests roughly SEK 1,000 per adult and says less still helps. The EU preparedness strategy calls for guidance towards at least 72 hours of self-sufficiency. These are local preparedness measures, not a coordinated withdrawal instruction, a Singapore cash target or proof of an impending outage.

Grab bagTorch and spare batteries stored separately, whistle, first-aid kit, N95, essential medication and any childcare or other care supplies. Add a charged power bank, cable, small water bottle and snack if manageable. Keep it accessible in the dark. Never delay leaving a fire to collect it.
Waterproof folderCopies of identity and insurance documents; contacts, meeting place, medication names/doses, allergies and prescription copies. Add passport or dependent/caregiver papers only if relevant. Keep originals secure and an encrypted offline copy you can actually open. Leave PINs, passwords, recovery codes and full card details out of the bag.
Cash and payment fallbackKeep a modest mix of small SGD notes and coins based on your essential food, transport and pharmacy spending. Keep a physical card separately. Build within your budget; cash helps only where merchants can accept it. No need to withdraw savings or convert foreign figures.
Separate home supplyRotate ordinary drinking water and shelf-stable food, lighting, charging and hygiene supplies; account for dependants and pets. Keep the evacuation bag light.

Do today: pack what you already own for 10 minutes; spend 10 on copies and contacts; spend 10 testing the torch, carrying the bag and agreeing on a meeting point. Check batteries and expiry dates periodically. Singapore: 995 for fire or emergency ambulance, 999 for police. Verify alarming banking messages independently; never share one-time codes.
