Public feature · AI infrastructure financing

Debt can accelerate useful capacity; liquidity determines survivability

Debt is economically constructive when it brings productive infrastructure online before internally generated cash alone could fund it. The key question is not “debt or no debt”; it is whether asset life, contracted cash flow, completion risk and refinancing terms match the obligation.

LayerWhy debt can be productiveWhere the structure can failEvidence to verify
Compute and data centresGPU-backed loans and project vehicles can bring scarce compute online earlier; long-term customer contracts can support repayment.Technology obsolescence, construction delay, weak utilisation or a few customers controlling most cash flow can impair the collateral and refinancing case.Commissioning dates, utilisation, contract duration, renewal terms, customer concentration and unlevered project cash flow.
Power, grid, cooling and networksLong-lived enabling assets can serve multiple users and may retain value beyond one chip cycle.Permitting, interconnection, fuel, water and equipment delays can leave debt accruing before revenue begins.Permits, secured megawatts, grid connection, cooling design, construction milestones and take-or-pay contracts.
Private-credit structureAsset-backed facilities can match funding to a specific project and diversify capital beyond bank balance sheets.Short maturities, floating rates, weak covenants and concentrated lenders or customers can turn a viable asset into a liquidity failure.Yield, maturity ladder, interest coverage, covenant headroom, collateral haircuts and committed versus optional customer demand.
